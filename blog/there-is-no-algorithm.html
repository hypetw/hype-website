There Is No Algorithm. There's a Science.

By Tiago Carvalho · Published 5 November 2026 · Updated 5 November 2026

Clients tell us reach is a lottery, and we understand why they believe it. Social media started as a place for your cat, your cousins and your lunch. It isn't that place anymore. Here's the ranking system nobody can see, the four faces it wears inside Instagram, and the Meta machine behind all of them.

Every month, a business owner tells us some version of the same thing: reach is a lottery. One post does numbers, the next one dies, and anyone claiming there's a science to it is selling something.

We understand exactly why that feels true, because of where all this started. Social media began as friend media. Your cat, your cousins, your dinner, your holiday: untouched subjects, shown in order, to people who knew you. Nothing ranked you, because nothing needed to. The feed was a fridge door and everyone's drawings went up.

That place doesn't exist anymore. Somewhere along the way the fridge door became a cinema with a very opinionated programmer, and the programme changed from who you know to what you care about. Interest media ranks your content against everything else your audience might care about, every time you post. Adam Mosseri said it himself last month: "more and more of the reach on Instagram came from recommendations", meaning strangers chosen by interest, not friends scrolling out of loyalty.

So the lottery feeling is real, but it's misdiagnosed. Your posts aren't being ignored. They're being ranked. And anything that ranks can be studied. That's not a sales pitch. That's the definition of a science.

There is no algorithm. There are four.

Here's the part we put in this week's carousel, because it's the single most useful correction in social media marketing: "the algorithm" doesn't exist. Inside Instagram alone there are four ranking systems, one per surface, each weighing different things, each doing a different job.

Feed mostly serves accounts people already follow. Stories goes narrower again: the people they're closest to. Reels is the entertainment engine, often filled by accounts the viewer doesn't follow yet. Explore is pure discovery, new accounts and new interests. Your reach depends on where a post lands and who is on the other side.

Read that again as a business owner. When your beautiful Reel "flopped," it didn't fail some cosmic dice roll. It was entered into the strangers lottery while you judged it by your followers' response, or the reverse. Most "the algorithm hates me" stories are just a post sent to the wrong face of it.

And what gets a post recommended to strangers isn't hidden either. Mosseri has named the weightiest signals twice now: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends", each measured per reach, with sends (would someone privately share this with a friend?) the heavy one for reaching people who don't follow you. Add the recommendation filters: originality gets more room than reposting, timely beats stale, and smaller accounts get deliberately shown to non-followers. The machine is a word-of-mouth detector with a stated preference for original work, and it publishes its own rules. Some science is hard to find. This one does interviews.

One more operator's note, dear to us: Mosseri said in October that photos can still grow an account without a single Reel, and that carousels "tend to do better than single photos" because they give a post multiple chances to be seen. The format this post is paired with isn't a house quirk. It's a ranking advantage, confirmed by the man who runs the ranking.

One idea, four faces

This is why "we posted it" is not a strategy. A company that understands the four faces doesn't make one piece of content and hope. It makes one idea and dresses it per surface:

  • The Reel carries the idea to strangers: fast, entertaining, built for watch time and the send button.
  • The carousel in Feed teaches it properly, with the multiple-chances advantage doing the ranking work.
  • Stories make it personal for the people already close to you: the behind-it, the vote, the reply box.
  • Explore and recommendations take care of themselves when the other three are original enough to earn sends.

Same thinking, produced once, ranked four times, each version native to its face. Not four copies. Four variations. That's the difference between content that compounds and the borrowed-format treadmill we wrote about in the trends piece.

The nutshell: Instagram's four faces live in Meta's house

Now zoom out once, because the four rankers inside Instagram are themselves one wing of a larger building, and this is the layer almost every Irish business ignores.

Meta has spent two years wiring its apps together. Cross-posting from Instagram and Facebook to Threads is a composer toggle, with auto-share available. Threads itself was bootstrapped off your Instagram graph: sign up with your account, your following carries over, and it now holds 500 million monthly users, within touching distance of X. And Facebook, the platform every strategy meeting smirks at, still covers 79% of the Irish population, with 25-to-34-year-olds its biggest age group. Not pensioners. The people booking tables and viewing houses. We covered in the reach-rationing piece why every extra original shot on goal matters now; the Meta estate is the cheapest version of that maths, because the extra shots are the same ball.

So the full picture, in a nutshell: your caption's sharpest line becomes the Threads post. The fuller story goes to Facebook, where links, longer text and the local groups actually work. The carousel teaches on Instagram across its four faces. One idea, produced once, entered into every ranking lottery the landlord runs. Your competitors are buying one ticket and calling the draw rigged.

Assemble when it's time to boost

Everything above is organic, and organic comes first, because organic is the laboratory: it tells you, in real numbers, which idea earns sends, which face it wins on, and which version of it strangers choose to watch. Most businesses run ads the other way around: boost the post the owner likes, aimed at everyone, shrug at the result. That's paying to distribute a guess.

The right order is the Avengers order. Each piece does its own job all year: the Reel out recruiting, the carousel teaching, Stories holding the regulars, Threads arguing, Facebook working the parish. And when a campaign moment arrives (a launch, a season, an offer), you assemble: take the proven performer, the one the organic data already crowned, and put Meta's ad system behind it, where one campaign can run across Facebook, Instagram and the rest of the estate at once. Ads don't replace the science. Ads are the science, scaled. The boost goes to the post that earned it, aimed at the audience the organic run revealed, timed to when it matters.

That's the whole discipline our three-part model exists for: Strategy decides what you stand for and which faces need what. Creation makes it original and juicy enough to rank everywhere, organically. And Amplification assembles the team when it's time to be loud, with budget behind proof instead of hope.

Reach stopped being a lottery the day the feed stopped being a fridge door. There's a ranking system with published rules, four faces inside Instagram, a connected estate around it, and an ad engine waiting for whatever you prove organically. None of that is luck. It's a science, and the businesses treating it like one are quietly taking the reach everyone else is praying for.
